# 2025: round the world in 300 days

At the start of 2025, my wife and I put our lives in Bend on pause to do some bucket-list travel.
We're calling it a little stint of "midlife retirement":
doing the trips at the edge of our comfort zone for this stage of life.

## January and February

We said goodbye to our life in Bend of the past five years,
sold off nearly everything,
and put the sentimental things in storage for our return.
We now own zero cars and four backpacks.
We'll certainly miss the great folks there, and the view from home.

## April

We took a five-day "layover" in the Dominican Republic:
long beach walks,
swimming in crystal clear cave water,
one of the most adrenaline-filled rafting trips we've done, on the river near Jarabacoa,
and a lesson in hand-rolling cigars.

Then we met up with two friends to walk the [Camino de Costa Rica](/blog/2025/apr/29/camino-de-costa-rica-recap/),
crossing from the Caribbean to the Pacific in just two weeks.
Along the way we saw baby sloths, several kinds of monkeys, and all the tropical birds,
and hiked for hours with the scent of coffee fields, sugarcane, bananas, and dates.
The highlight was our hosts.
Each night we were the only guests of a local Costa Rican family,
who usually made our meals and told us stories of the trail and the area.
Some doting grandmother types kept us stuffed with home cooking, local fruit, and even homemade chocolates.

## May

I ran Write the Docs Portland, which had over 400 attendees,
then went to PyCon in Pittsburgh,
and spent a week cycling around Amsterdam and the Netherlands with friends.

## June

We spent time in Scotland and Ireland,
with weather-worn scenery and lively music in cozy pubs.
Then we traveled to Montenegro, by way of Dubrovnik and a ferry to Kotor,
and started our hiking season in the fantastic rock landscapes of Durmitor National Park.

## July

We walked the Peaks of the Balkans trail,
which crosses the borders between Montenegro, Albania, and Kosovo,
and then the Via Alpina, across Switzerland.

We got rained out partway across Switzerland,
and found that the local train ran straight to Milan.
We had a nice week waiting out the weather and eating all the gelato, pasta, and pizza.
Back on the trail we struggled with lots of elevation gain,
sometimes 6,000 feet up and down in a day.
Luckily there were gondolas when we needed them,
to save our knees while we took in the stunning Alps.

## August

We finished the Via Alpina,
then went back to favorite spots where we wanted more time: Kandersteg and Grindelwald.
Each morning we woke to cowbells,
and we resupplied with cheese from the alp dairies.

After a reset in Munich we started the Carnic Alps High Route,
along a high ridge on the Austrian-Italian border.
It was a highlight of the summer,
with sweeping views of the Dolomites and great mountain huts.

To wrap up the summer we took a short trip to Slovenia:
hiking in Triglav National Park, Lake Bled, and Ljubljana.

## September

Before we ran out of time in Europe we did one last hike, the Alta Via 1:
rugged walking through the heart of the Dolomites,
starting in an area we'd visited briefly in 2018.

After a stop in Venice we flew to Istanbul,
and were welcomed by the call to prayer from mosques masterfully lit at night.
It felt like stepping into a new world.

A bucket-list item was a safari in Kenya.
The Maasai Mara really is incredible:
just inside the gate we could count over a dozen species of wildlife.
We had close encounters with lions, and later with elephants in Amboseli.

## October

After the safari we went to Diani Beach,
which had more unique and spectacular wildlife.

We also organized our own trip to the remote Kakamega rainforest.
Getting there on local transport was a challenge,
but the reward was days of walking the reserve with only our guide,
and being the only guests in a house next to misty tea fields.

Then we went to Turkey's beautiful Mediterranean coast for some diving and relaxing,
before I left for Berlin to hold Write the Docs' first European conference since 2019.

## December

Then Japan.
The bullet trains and temples are amazing.
Kyoto was a favorite, with temples tucked all over the surrounding hills,
and matcha and onigiri became a daily ritual.
Hiroshima was sobering,
but the Peace Museum gave us the space we needed for reflection.

## By the numbers

- **0** cars and **4** backpacks.
- **18** countries outside the US, from Salt Cay to Japan.
- **165** miles and **30,000** feet of climbing on the Camino de Costa Rica, in **14** days.
- **6,000** feet up and down on the biggest days of the Via Alpina.
- **4** treks, through the Balkans, Switzerland, the Carnic Alps, and the Dolomites.
- **2** Write the Docs conferences, in Portland and Berlin.
- **1** week of gelato, pasta, and pizza in Milan, courtesy of the rain.

## The 2025 awards

- **Best hosts:** the families who took us in each night on the Camino de Costa Rica, and fed us homemade chocolates.
- **Best rain delay:** a week in Milan, when the train from the Via Alpina ran straight there.
- **Best alarm clock:** cowbells in Kandersteg and Grindelwald.
- **Best hut-to-hut walk:** the Carnic Alps High Route, with the Dolomites in view the whole way.
- **Hardest days:** the climbs on the Via Alpina.
- **Best rescue:** the gondolas that saved our knees.
- **Hardest to get to:** the Kakamega rainforest, by local transport.
- **Most adrenaline:** rafting the river near Jarabacoa.
- **Best daily ritual:** matcha and onigiri in Kyoto.

## Who we met

The wildlife list for the year, in order of appearance:

- **Salt Cay:** baby humpback whales, and flamingos.
- **Costa Rica:** baby sloths, several kinds of monkeys, and a quetzal.
- **The Alps:** a lot of cows, with bells.
- **Kenya:** lions and giraffes in the Maasai Mara, and elephants in Amboseli.
- **More of Kenya:** black-and-white colobus monkeys and a bushbaby.
- **Japan:** snow monkeys in Nagano.

## Looking back

Lots to be grateful for this past year.
We've covered a lot of ground, met amazing new people,
and loved sharing parts of it with friends and family.
Travel can be exhilarating, exhausting, and isolating, depending on the day.
But each day we wake up feeling fortunate to be alive,
and able to experience the vast diversity of people and places the world has to offer.

In 2026 we'll be in Asia for the spring, focusing on scuba diving.
I'll be back in Oregon for Write the Docs in May,
and then we're planning some long-distance hiking in the US this summer.
